# The Amplifier

> **VOICE. VOLUME. JUSTICE.**

The Amplifier’s official motto. It should function as a recurring piece of character branding across the website, comic covers, name badges, posters, and other in-world material.

---

## Character Overview

**The Amplifier** is a comic-book superhero built around an exaggerated office persona: curious, confident, relentlessly inquisitive, and famously loud.

He does not whisper. He does not mumble. He does not need to repeat himself.

When The Amplifier has a question, everyone hears it.

His defining joke is that he carries a microphone and portable amplifier as if they are essential superhero equipment, even though his natural voice is already powerful enough to stop criminals, scatter paperwork, rattle windows, and silence an entire room.

The equipment is mostly theatrical.

His voice is the real superpower.

---

## Core Concept

The Amplifier turns ordinary workplace traits into absurd superhero abilities.

He is the person who:

- asks the question everyone else was avoiding;
- notices an assumption that does not quite make sense;
- follows up when an answer is vague;
- raises the energy in the room;
- and, above all, speaks at a volume that makes amplification technically unnecessary.

His adventures should combine **classic comic-book heroism** with **mundane, low-stakes problems treated with ridiculous dramatic intensity**.

A bank robbery deserves a sonic blast.

So does an overdue library book.

---

## Visual Identity

The Amplifier should look agile and energetic rather than enormous or heavily muscled. His physique is closer to a nimble, athletic superhero than a traditional powerhouse.

### Appearance

- Lean, athletic build.
- Brown hair.
- No glasses.
- Expressive face, usually caught mid-question, mid-declaration, or mid-shout.
- A black superhero-style eye mask may be used as part of the comic-book identity.

### Costume

His costume deliberately mixes superhero imagery with office clothing.

- Crisp **white buttoned shirt**.
- **Black tie** with a small **skull icon at the tip**.
- Dark trousers.
- Black gloves.
- Green cape or other green superhero accents.
- Utility straps or harness for carrying audio equipment.
- Handheld microphone.
- Portable amplifier or speaker.

The shirt stays properly buttoned. The joke comes from the contrast between professional office clothing and exaggerated superhero action, rather than a traditional bare-chested superhero look.

---

## Name Badge

One of the recurring visual jokes is The Amplifier's office-style name badge.

His primary badge can carry the short superhero motto:

> **THE AMPLIFIER**  
> **VOICE. VOLUME. JUSTICE.**

A more detailed office-style version can read:

> **HELLO**  
> **I'M**  
> **THE AMPLIFIER**  
> *ASKS QUESTIONS.*  
> *RAISES VOLUME.*  
> *SOLVES PROBLEMS.*

Both versions belong to the character. **“Voice. Volume. Justice.”** is the punchy public-facing slogan; **“Asks questions. Raises volume. Solves problems.”** is the more explanatory workplace joke.

The badge should remain visible in character art whenever practical. It helps explain the character immediately and reinforces the workplace-superhero concept.

## Powers

### Sonic Voice

The Amplifier can project his voice with impossible force.

At full volume, his voice can create visible sound waves powerful enough to:

- knock criminals off balance;
- stop fleeing suspects;
- rattle doors and windows;
- send loose papers flying;
- interrupt getaway vehicles;
- overwhelm a room;
- and make extremely minor administrative announcements feel like world-ending proclamations.

His sound waves should generally be shown as originating from **his mouth**, not from the speaker.

### Questioning

The Amplifier's less literal superpower is his ability to ask exactly the question that exposes a weakness in a plan.

He is particularly dangerous to:

- villains with poorly considered schemes;
- people making unsupported assumptions;
- unnecessary complexity;
- vague answers;
- and anyone who says, "We've always done it this way."

### Presence

The Amplifier does not enter a room quietly.

Even before he uses his powers, people know he is there. His confidence, energy, and volume naturally turn him into the centre of a scene.

---

## Equipment

### The Microphone

The Amplifier carries a classic handheld microphone.

He often raises it dramatically before making an announcement, issuing a warning, or questioning a criminal.

The running joke is that the microphone contributes almost nothing.

### The Amplifier

He also carries a portable speaker or amplifier.

It may be strapped to him, carried like a piece of tactical equipment, or placed nearby depending on the scene.

Despite being his namesake, the device is not the source of his power.

His voice is.

The equipment exists because The Amplifier believes every dramatic entrance can be improved by additional audio hardware.

---

## Personality

The Amplifier is not grim or intimidating by nature. He is enthusiastic, inquisitive, energetic, and slightly theatrical.

He genuinely wants to solve problems.

His defining personality traits are:

- **Curious** — he always wants to understand why.
- **Direct** — he asks the question rather than dancing around it.
- **Energetic** — every problem feels worth engaging with.
- **Confident** — he assumes the question deserves to be heard.
- **Loud** — not occasionally loud; constitutionally loud.
- **Good-humoured** — the character works best when he takes absurd situations seriously without becoming cruel.

He should feel more like an overenthusiastic problem-solver than an aggressive enforcer.

---

## Comic Tone

The world of The Amplifier should operate on the logic of a superhero comic, but the problems do not always deserve superhero treatment.

That contrast is the central comedy engine.

Examples:

- A bank robbery is stopped by one enormous command to **"FREEZE!"**
- A getaway driver is forced to stop after The Amplifier bellows **"PULL OVER!"**
- A villain's complicated plan collapses after he asks one inconvenient question.
- A library patron returns a book one week late and receives the full force of The Amplifier announcing: **"THAT'LL BE A 50c FINE!"**

The more ordinary the offence, the funnier the heroic overreaction can become.

---

## The Library Incident

One of the defining early scenes for the character takes place inside a traditional library.

A nervous patron has returned a book **one week late**.

The room is filled with signs saying things such as:

- **QUIET PLEASE**
- **SILENCE PLEASE**
- **SILENCE IS GOLDEN**
- **SHHH!**

The Amplifier stands in the middle of this carefully maintained quiet environment wearing his white shirt, black skull-tipped tie, green cape, gloves, name badge, and audio equipment.

He opens his mouth.

Visible sonic waves explode across the library.

Books tremble. Patrons recoil. Librarians stare in horror. The overdue borrower clutches the offending book.

The Amplifier announces:

> **"THAT'LL BE A 50c FINE!"**

This scene captures the character perfectly: enormous superhero energy applied to an absurdly minor problem, with complete confidence that the situation demanded it.

## Catchphrases and Dialogue

The Amplifier's dialogue should be short, declarative, and easy to imagine shouted across a room.

Possible recurring lines include:

- **"I HAVE A FEW QUESTIONS!"**
- **"CAN EVERYONE HEAR ME?!"**
- **"LET'S CIRCLE BACK!"**
- **"PUT THAT BACK!"**
- **"STOP RIGHT THERE!"**
- **"NO FURTHER QUESTIONS!"**
- **"THAT'LL BE A 50c FINE!"**

His questions can sometimes function like attacks: once he starts asking them, a villain's plan begins to unravel.

## Recurring Gags

### He Does Not Need the Equipment

Characters regularly notice that The Amplifier's microphone and speaker are unnecessary.

This should never stop him from carrying them.

### Quiet Signs

Whenever possible, place The Amplifier near signs requesting silence, low volume, calm behaviour, or indoor voices.

### Collateral Paperwork

His sonic blasts frequently send forms, receipts, reports, sticky notes, overdue notices, or office paperwork flying through the air.

### Excessive Escalation

Tiny infractions receive the visual treatment normally reserved for supervillain confrontations.

### Questions as Weapons

Villains may survive the sonic blast only to be defeated by a follow-up question they cannot answer.

---

## Possible Origin Story

The Amplifier's origin should remain slightly ridiculous.

One possible version:

He was once an ordinary office worker known for asking useful questions and speaking at a volume normally associated with public-address systems.

During a mysterious incident involving experimental acoustic technology, a microphone, and a meeting that had already gone fifteen minutes over time, his natural voice became superhuman.

He discovered that he could project sound with extraordinary force.

Rather than abandon his old habits, he did what came naturally:

He bought a bigger microphone.

And an amplifier.

Neither was necessary.

---

## Enemies and Story Hooks

The Amplifier's villains can parody workplace behaviour, bad communication, and unnecessary complexity while still looking like proper comic-book antagonists.

### The Silencer

A villain obsessed with suppressing discussion, disagreement, and inconvenient questions.

### The Obfuscator

A master of jargon who can make any simple idea incomprehensible.

### Doctor Scope Creep

Every plan becomes larger, slower, and more complicated in his presence.

### The Meeting Master

A villain capable of trapping entire teams in meetings with no agenda and no clear end.

### Captain Assumption

Builds elaborate criminal schemes on things nobody ever bothered to verify.

### The Echo Chamber

Can make a group hear only opinions they already agree with.

These villains give The Amplifier a reason to use both of his signature abilities: **volume and questions**.

## Website Storytelling Direction

A website about The Amplifier could present his world as if he were a completely legitimate comic-book hero.

The comedy works best when the site itself takes him seriously.

Possible sections include:

The phrase **“VOICE. VOLUME. JUSTICE.”** should be treated as a recurring visual motif — ideal for the website masthead, comic-cover lockups, loading screens, case-file headers, or a mock superhero seal.

1. **Hero Profile** — character introduction, powers, costume, equipment, and personality.
2. **Origin Story** — how an already-loud office personality became a sonic superhero.
3. **Case Files** — individual illustrated stories such as the Library Incident, bank robbery, museum heist, or getaway chase.
4. **Villains** — profiles of recurring enemies such as The Silencer and Doctor Scope Creep.
5. **The Amplifier's Questions** — short comic panels built around absurdly incisive questions.
6. **Sound Level** — a humorous power meter ranging from "indoor voice" to "quarterly planning session."
7. **Equipment** — lovingly over-detailed descriptions of microphones and amplifiers that he demonstrably does not need.
8. **Gallery** — comic covers, action scenes, fake advertisements, and character art.

---

## Character Summary

**The Amplifier** is a lean, brown-haired office-worker-turned-superhero who fights crime with curiosity, confidence, and an impossibly loud voice.

He wears a crisp white shirt, black skull-tipped tie, dark trousers, black gloves, green superhero accents, and an office name badge proudly identifying him as **THE AMPLIFIER**.

He carries a microphone and portable amplifier everywhere.

Neither is necessary.

His greatest weapons are his voice and the question nobody else thought to ask.

**Voice. Volume. Justice.**

And when justice requires it, everyone is going to hear about it.
